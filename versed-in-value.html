Three kinds of value, one set of principles

Most advice treats money, career and ethics as separate subjects with separate rules. In my experience they behave more like one system: the habit that grows your savings is the same one that builds expertise and trust, and a shortcut that looks cheap in one area tends to cost you in another.

I work in finance and spent a year in internal audit, where the job is checking whether things are worth what they claim to be. I don't have all the answers, but I want to bring that same scrutiny here. Every principle is read three ways:

Economic value

What is this worth? The difference between price, what you pay, and value, what you get.

Professional value

What problem do I solve? Being valued for the problems you solve rather than the hours you put in.

Ethical value

What do I stand for? The filter that stops a short-term gain from costing you long-term trust.

Twelve principles, one a month

Each month takes one principle and follows it through all three kinds of value: what it means, how to use it, a real case where it held or failed, and where it stops being true.
